# Content and Safety Decisions

- The opening screen shows the emergency direction and demonstration-only disclosure before the user can begin. A shorter emergency reminder appears beside the experience and chronology questions.
- The flow asks what was observed rather than asking what Castelzor caused. Symptom examples are explicitly not confirmed side effects.
- Serious outcomes are recorded as the reporter describes them. Selecting one does not diagnose, block the flow, or tell the person whether an event is safe. The emergency reminder remains visible.
- Unknown onset and product dates are valid answers. Dose, route, end date, and lot information are optional; lot details can explicitly be unknown or unavailable.
- The Castelzor route is treated as fixed, so the form does not ask how it was taken. The stop-date controls distinguish “still taking” from “I’m not sure” and clear conflicting stop-date values when either is selected.
- Selecting “Other symptom or problem” reveals a required text field. Unchecking it clears that field, and the review reflects only selected answers. Other answers persist when moving backward and forward.
- Reporter name and contact details are clearly fictional demo fields. Completion is a local state change and does not create a case number or transmit information.
- The closing screen leads with “No report was sent,” then separately describes a hypothetical real Castelzor safety-team process without implying that it occurred here.
- No health or contact answers enter local storage, URLs, browser history, analytics, or a server. Reloading and Start over both clear the reactive draft.
